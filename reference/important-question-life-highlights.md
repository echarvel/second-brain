- Category:: #articles
- Summary:: In this article, the author discusses the importance of considering what pain one is willing to endure in life rather than solely focusing on what one desires. The author argues that the quality of one's life is determined by the quality of the negative experiences they are willing to sustain. They give examples of various areas of life, such as career, relationships, and physical fitness, where individuals must be willing to embrace the struggle in order to achieve their goals. The author emphasizes that it is crucial to choose struggles that align with one's values and to understand that the process is just as important as the end result.

  - If I ask you, “What do you want out of life?” and you say something like, “I want to [be happy](https://markmanson.net/how-to-be-happy) and have a great family and a job I like,” it’s so ubiquitous it doesn’t even mean anything.

A more interesting question—a question that perhaps you’ve never considered before—is what pain do you want in your life? What are you willing to struggle for? Because that seems to be a greater determinant of how our lives turn out.

Everybody wants to have an [amazing job](https://markmanson.net/how-to-quit-your-job-and-travel-the-world) and financial independence—but not everyone wants to suffer through 60-hour work weeks, long commutes, and obnoxious paperwork, to navigate arbitrary corporate hierarchies and the blasé confines of an infinite cubicle hell. People want to be rich without [the risk](https://markmanson.net/risk), without the sacrifice, without the [delayed gratification](https://markmanson.net/delayed-gratification) necessary to [accumulate wealth](https://markmanson.net/the-real-value-of-money).[1](https://markmanson.net/question?_ke=cjR6cXNydTR6a3NvZHlsZWxhQHRocm90dC5jb20%3D#footnote-1)

Everybody wants to have great sex and an [awesome relationship](https://markmanson.net/fuck-yes)—but not everyone is willing to go through the tough conversations, the awkward silences, the hurt feelings, and the emotional psychodrama to get there.

They view pain as an objectively negative thing to be avoided at all costs, whereas reality is much more nuanced. As I cover extensively in my [Resilience Course](https://markmanson.net/subscribe/resilience-signup?from=question) in the [Mark Manson Premium Subscription](https://markmanson.net/subscribe?from=question), we are all capable of—and I’d argue responsible for—ascribing meaning to our pain, and this can actually give our life purpose.
