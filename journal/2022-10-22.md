- #[[relevant-notes]]#purpose-of-meditation
  - #[[permanent-notes]][[purpose-meditation-allows-stop-being-identified|The purpose of meditation allows is to stop being identified with the contents of your mind. It is not to suppress thoughts or emotions, but to be able to observe them clearly as they come into our consciousness.]]
    - #fleeting-notes Meditate not to stop thinking, or to prevent interruptions, but to observe your thoughts with clarity. To go one level above just having thoughts interrupt you without any control. With practice, you'll learn how to observe and let go of those thoughts and recognize and filter the valuable thoughts from the irrational.
    - #literature-notesThe goal isn't to stop your thoughts or to suppress any emotion that might arise with it, it is rather to notice these mental events clearly and to experience them fully, more fully in fact, to recognize them as appearances in consciousness as they arise (Harris, Meditation 2, Introductory Course)
    - #literature-notes Whether the experience is pleasant or unpleasant isn't' really the point... you're not adding an artifice to your experience in the present moment, you're becoming less distracted. The purpose of meditation is to discover what your mind is like when you're no longer perpetually identified with the contents of your thoughts (Harris, Meditation 2, Introductory Course).

  - #literature-notes On keeping a tidy home
    - Make your bed each morning. Mess attracts mess. One of the easiest places to see this is the bedroom. Your bed is the centerpiece of the room, and if it is left unmade, clutter begins to accumulate around it. The first, best step when cleaning a bedroom, then, is to make the bed. And the first, best step for everyday clutter-free living is to make the bed first thing in the morning. Becker, Joshua. The More of Less (p. 139). The Crown Publishing Group. Kindle Edition.
    - Wash dishes right away. Hand washing some dishes takes less time than putting them in the dishwasher. This applies to cups, breakfast bowls, dinner plates, and silverware. If you hand-wash right after eating, it will take hardly any time at all. If, however, hand washing is not an option, be sure to put used dishes in the dishwasher right away. Nobody likes walking into a kitchen with dishes piled up in the sink or on the counter, and it’s even less fun eating in there. Becker, Joshua. The More of Less (p. 139). The Crown Publishing Group. Kindle Edition.
    - Fill your recycling containers and garbage containers. Use every trash pick-up day as an excuse to fill your recycling receptacle and garbage can. Grab a box of junk from the attic, broken toys from the playroom, spoiled food from the pantry, outdated paperwork from the office — whatever has built up. Then put it where the trash person will pick it up. You’ll quickly get the hang of this. You may even begin to look forward to trash day. (Okay, maybe I shouldn’t go that far.) Becker, Joshua. The More of Less (p. 140). The Crown Publishing Group. Kindle Edition.
    - Always leave room in your coat closet. There’s a good reason why coats, boots, and outerwear end up scattered throughout your home. It’s because your coat closet is so full that it’s a hassle to put things away and retrieve them quickly. So leave room on the coat-closet floor, on the hangers, and on the shelves for members of your family to quickly put away or retrieve items.
    - Keep flat surfaces clear. Kitchen counters, bathroom counters, bedroom dressers, tabletops, desktops — these are areas that just naturally collect clutter. Put small kitchen appliances away. Scoop up coins. File receipts.  Becker, Joshua. The More of Less (p. 140). The Crown Publishing Group. Kindle Edition.
    - Complete one- to two-minute jobs immediately. Clutter is often a result of procrastination — decisions put off or small jobs left unfinished. Counteract this procrastination in your home with a simple rule: if a job can be completed in less than two minutes, do it now. Take the garbage out, scrub the pot, return the remote control, or place your dirty clothes in the hamper. Every time you see a task all the way through to completion, you’ve forestalled the development of clutter. Becker, Joshua. The More of Less (p. 141). The Crown Publishing Group. Kindle Edition.
    - When you finish a magazine or newspaper, process it immediately. Good recipe in there? Put it in your recipe box and recycle the rest. An article that your husband will enjoy? Clip it and recycle. Coupon too good to pass up? Cut it out and recycle. Stacks of magazines and newspapers serve little purpose but to clutter a room. Becker, Joshua. The More of Less (p. 141). The Crown Publishing Group. Kindle Edition.
    - Take care of clothes immediately. When it came to clothes, I used to be a throw-them-on-the-floor guy. Now I handle each item right when I take it off. Dirty clothes down the laundry chute. Clean clothes back to the hanger or drawer. That’s it. Becker, Joshua. The More of Less (p. 141). The Crown Publishing Group. Kindle Edition.
    - Nightly, return items where they belong. Tell your kids to put their toys away at the end of every day. You need to do the same with the items you’re responsible for. Just make a sweep of the house, grab whatever misplaced items you see, and stash them in their places. Do this every night without fail, and it will allow you to begin each morning in a house that’s fresh, clean, and clutter-free. Becker, Joshua. The More of Less (p. 142). The Crown Publishing Group. Kindle Edition.
- #[[relevant-notes]]#why-excess-is-evil
  - #literature-notesWhen we eat unhealthily, we miss an opportunity to fuel our bodies properly. When we watch too much TV or spend too much time online, we miss opportunities to interact with people in the real world. When we neglect to exercise, we miss the opportunity to enjoy the kinds of adventures available to those with physical stamina. When we stay up late and sleep through the morning, we may be missing out on the most productive period of our day. When we buy more than we need, we miss the opportunity to live free and unburdened. When we spend more than we earn, we shackle ourselves with bondage to debt. When we spend too much money on ourselves, we miss the opportunity to find greater joy by being generous to others. Becker, Joshua. The More of Less (pp. 191-192). The Crown Publishing Group. Kindle Edition.
