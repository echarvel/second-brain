# July 23rd, 2025

- #fleeting-notes Here's why Keret's stories are often labeled as absurdist:
  - Surreal and Fantastical Elements: Keret frequently introduces bizarre and illogical elements into everyday situations. His characters encounter talking fish that grant wishes, [a woman who unzips her boyfriend to reveal a German gentile inside](https://www.theparisreview.org/blog/2012/05/01/something-out-of-something-talking-with-etgar-keret/), or a man who is kidnapped and taken to his own childhood. These departures from reality highlight the irrationality and unexpected turns of life.
  - Juxtaposition of the Mundane and the Extraordinary: His stories often place ordinary characters in extraordinary or absurd situations, emphasizing the strangeness of the world and the human experience within it.
  - Exploration of the Human Condition in a Confusing World: Keret's works confront the difficulties and paradoxes of human existence, reflecting the anxieties of modern life and the challenges of finding meaning in an often chaotic world. [The Tufts Daily notes](https://www.tuftsdaily.com/article/2018/11/etgar-keret-based-true-story-exhibits-beauty-absurdity-storytelling) that he uses his fiction to confront sadness and offer comfort through humor.
  - Humor and Wit: Despite the underlying seriousness of the themes, Keret's stories are often infused with dark humor and wit, which is a characteristic element of absurdist literature. [The Guardian describes](https://www.theguardian.com/books/2025/jul/02/autocorrect-by-etgar-keret-review-endlessly-inventive-short-stories) his humor as a "wan metaphysical wit".
  - Conciseness and Focus: His very short stories often pack a powerful punch, using brevity to emphasize the key absurdist elements and leave a lasting impression.
  - While some might also categorize his work as magical realism, surrealism, or simply speculative fiction, the label of "absurdist" effectively captures the core of his philosophical perspective on the world. He uses the absurd and fantastical as a means to explore the deeper realities and complexities of the human condition, particularly within the context of Israeli society, [according to The Paris Review](https://www.theparisreview.org/blog/2012/05/01/something-out-of-something-talking-with-etgar-keret/).
