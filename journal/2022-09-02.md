- 08:52#[[relevant-notes]]#smarter-goals
  - #[[permanent-notes]][[use-smarter-acronym-guide-set-annual-goals|Use the SMARTER acronym as a guide to set Annual goals (Specific, Measurable, Actionable, Risky, Time-keyed, Exciting and Relevant) . They should start with a verb and be stated positively.]]
  - #literature-notes Set seven to ten goals you want to achieve for the year. Make them SMARTER:
    - **Specific**. Focus is power. You can drive the same amount of water through two pipes and create greater force in one of them just by reducing its diameter. That’s similar to what happens when we narrow our goals.
    - **Measurable**. They have built-in criteria you can measure yourself against.
    - **Actionable**. Use a strong verb to prompt the action you want to take.
    - **Risky**. But by focusing on what’s supposedly realistic, we can inadvertently trigger our natural impulse to avoid loss and end up accomplishing less than we otherwise might have. I’m not saying we should set goals that are crazy. I am saying we should set goals that stretch and challenge us.
    - **Time-keyed**. This could be a deadline, frequency, or a time trigger. They can be a deadline a streak target, a start date, a time trigger or a habit frequency.
    - **Exciting**. The goal should inspire you and be internally motivating.
    - **Relevant**. This is about alignment, and it comes at the end of the list because it’s a good way to gut-check your goals before committing to them.

  - #[[permanent-notes]][[several-things-measure-make-sure-going-achieve|There are several things we can measure to make sure we are going to achieve our goals. If you follow SMARTER or PACT guidelines, then your goals will have indicators to measure. At a tactical level, meaning quarterly goals divided into weekly tasks, we should be measuring the percentage of tasks completed during the week, and the percentage of days that we are following through on our habits.
