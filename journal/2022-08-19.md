    - We carry around a lot of unresolved issues which doesn't allow us to reinvent our future. We nurse grievances to justify current actions, or feel unvalued because we were slighted in some way. Completing the past is an essential part of designing a better life, and for that we need to acknowledge what we've already experienced instead of avoiding unpleasant memories.
      - In order to look forward, we need to look backward and analyze our past. We need to state what happened, and compare it against what we wanted to happen, thus learning from the experience. Given this analysis, we can then adjust our behavior.
      - Regret can be paralyzing, but it can also be used to change. There are three benefits of regret. First, regret is a form of information that reflects our missteps. Two, it gives us a motivation to change. Finally, it gives us integrity, the moral compass to guide us to the correct path. Use regret to deal with the pass in a positive way.
      - is another way to deal with the past. It changes the focus from what is wrong with our past, to the things for which we are grateful. Praying, expressing gratitude and keeping a gratitude journal can help us shift this focus.
    - One of the reasons we don't succeed with our goals is that we doubt we can. Some of our limiting beliefs come from previous failures or setbacks. Others come from our negativity bias, which has more intensity with some people than others. In the end, we get to choose what we're going to believe, and the best way to overcome limiting beliefs is to replace them with liberating truths (Michael Hyatt)
      - Limiting beliefs can be about the world, about ourselves and about others. We can identify several types of limiting beliefs:
        - Black-and-white thinking. That’s when we assume we’ve failed if we don’t achieve perfection. Reality is usually a sliding scale, not a toggle switch.
        - Personalizing. That’s when we blame ourselves for random negative occurrences.
        - Catastrophizing. That’s when we assume the worst even with little evidence.2 To that list we can add a fourth:
        - Universalizing. That’s when we take a bad experience and assume it’s true across the board.
    - Four properties that help us achieve our goals (Michael Hyatt)
      - The first is intention. We can imagine a better reality than the one we’re currently experiencing. And we can work with others and within our circumstances to achieve it.
      - Second, forethought. By visualizing the future, we can govern our behavior in the present and give purpose and meaning to our actions.
      - Third, action. We have the ability to act on our plans, to stay motivated, and to respond in the moment to remain on course.
      - Finally, self-reflection. We not only act, we know we act. That means we can evaluate how we’re doing, make adjustments, and even revise our plans.
    - To upgrade your beliefs (Michael Hyatt):
      - Recognize the limiting belief.
      - Record the belief (write it down).
      - Review the belief, evaluate it's power over you.
      - Reject/Reframe the belief, either contradict it or turn the obstacle to an advantage.
      - Revise. Rewrite the belief in the form of a new liberating truth.
      - Reorient. Commit yourself to that new belief.
